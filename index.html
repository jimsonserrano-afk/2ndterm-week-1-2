    The one big idea

    An ecosystem can support only a limited number of individuals of one species. That limit is the carrying capacity (K). When a population is small, it grows fast. As it approaches K, competition for the scarcest resource slows growth until births and deaths balance.

    Four limiting factors

    • Food: each deer needs 1 meal per month, so Kfood = meals per month.
    • Water: Kwater = waterholes × 15 deer.
    • Shelter: Kshelter = thickets × 8 deer.
    • Mates: reproduction needs a male. At low density females struggle to find one, so growth starts slowly.

    Equations used in this app

    K = min(Kfood, Kwater, Kshelter)

    The scarcest resource is the bottleneck.

    P(mate) = 1 − e−M/h

    M = males in the herd; h = mate-finding difficulty.

    dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K)

    The textbook logistic model (Verhulst, 1838), drawn as a dotted line. Here r = 0.4 × ½ − 0.02 = 0.18 per month. Growth per month is greatest at N = K/2.

    How the simulation works

    Every month, each deer independently gets "enough" with probability s = 1 / (1 + (N/K)4). Deer that get enough die with probability 2%, and females among them have a 40% chance × P(mate) of a fawn. Deer that struggle die with probability 22% and do not breed.

    Honest note: the shape of s is a modeling assumption that represents competition. The species values are invented for teaching. Real populations also change with seasons, predators and disease.

    Connect it to the Philippines

    • Fish catch limits in coastal waters keep catches from exceeding what the fish population can regrow.
    • Community water supply sets how many households a barangay can sustain.
    • Land use: forests, farms and settlements compete for the same land and resources.

    Learning competencies

    • Discuss the factors that limit the ecosystem's carrying capacity, such as adequate food, shelter, water and mates.
    • Explain that the ecosystem's population growth slows down as it gets closer to the carrying capacity.
